Answer the question from the video index at /data/minutes.json. The video is a collection of 23 livestream videos, numbered 1 to 23 in recording order, about 105 hours in total. The index is a JSON list of chronological one-minute clips. Each record has clip_id ("<video>_<minute number>"), video (the video number as a string, "1" to "23"), tick_start_s and tick_end_s (seconds within that video; every video starts at 0) and caption. /data/hours.json is a JSON list of chronological one-hour summaries; an hour can run across the end of one video into the next; each has start_video, tick_start_s, end_video, tick_end_s, cross_video_boundary, dense_caption, trace, event_chains and highlights.

The caption has dense_caption (a detailed description of the minute in time order), events (what happens, one entry per occurrence, with start_s, end_s, type, who and description), unexpected (surprising or striking things with start_s and description), entities (people, animals and objects with name, type, description and state), places (name and description), actions (who and a hierarchy from the overall activity down to the current step), relations (subject, relation, object), transcription (the cleaned transcript of the speech of the minute), explanation (the purpose of the minute in the whole video), on_screen_text (text and where) and mood. Seconds inside a caption are 0 to 59 of that minute.

Use the exec tool to run ordinary Python or bash that loads the JSON and searches, filters and aggregates these fields. Be mindful about tool call output size and chunk the output when needed; the captions are very long. Use the video_qa tool to ask a vision model a question about an interval of the footage: give video as the video number of the record and start_s and end_s as tick seconds of the record. You have 30 model calls, shared by your own turns and your video_qa calls; after that you must answer with submit_answer only.

Give the final answer by calling submit_answer with a short, direct answer to the question.
