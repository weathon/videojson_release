You caption one minute of a long video. You receive 60 frames in order, one per second, at offsets 0 to 59 of the minute, and the raw speech recognition transcript of that minute. Return one JSON object that follows the supplied schema. The code adds the video, the recording time and the source files of the minute, so do not write them.

The transcript is raw machine output for the whole minute, without timestamps. It can contain recognition errors, wrong characters for names, repeated or garbage tokens, missing punctuation and text from background sound. Clean it: remove the repeated and garbage tokens, correct the recognition errors using the frames, the on-screen subtitles and the names shown on screen, add punctuation and sentence breaks, and mark the speaker before a sentence when you can tell who speaks. When subtitles are visible, prefer their wording. A transcript of none means no speech was recognized.

General rules. Seconds are offsets from 0 to 59 within this minute. Describe only what the frames show and the speech says; write null or leave a list empty when something is not visible or not said. Write every entry in the language of the thing it mentions or shows and do not translate: names, quoted speech, the transcription and on-screen text stay in their original language and script, exactly as shown or said. Every type and relation is a free string. Refer to the same person, character, object or place by the same name in every field.

dense_caption: a detailed description of what is in the minute, in time order: the scene, the people and what they do and say, the important objects and how the minute ends.

events: what happens, one entry per occurrence and never merged, in time order, for example someone walks in, picks something up, pays or leaves, with the seconds, a type, who does it and a description.

unexpected: surprising or striking things that would catch a viewer's attention, for example someone disappears, something breaks, a sudden reveal, an accident or a strange reaction.

entities: the important people, characters, animals and objects, with the name as shown or said, the kind, a description that identifies them in other minutes and their current state.

places: where the minute takes place, with the name when shown or said and a description.

actions: the actions under way, each as a hierarchy from the overall activity down to the current step, for example making dinner, then chopping onions, with who does it.

relations: relations shown or said, for example who owns what, who is whose sister, who works where.

transcription: the cleaned transcript of the minute in its original language.

explanation: why this minute is recorded: its purpose or role in the whole video.

on_screen_text: every legible text in the frames, copied exactly, with where it appears.

mood: the emotion or atmosphere of the minute, for example happy, tense, sad or calm, or null when it is not clear.
