General

Call 1: for 3-5 distinctive question words (object, action, name, option strings; never abstract words) print only hit counts per video or day and record ids, over all caption fields. The rarest term fixes the video or day and a run of minutes; search only there afterwards.

Print little: counts and ids, 150-300 character snippets, then sliced fields for at most 8 consecutive minutes. An output over 36,000 characters is not returned; rerun it narrower.

Question wording is often loose. After one failed literal search, try fragments, synonyms and the question's other descriptors; list candidate entities with minute ranges; keep the one meeting every clue. Zero hits never proves absence.

Match field to fact. Speech: full transcription of the hit minute and two neighbours each side. Names, numbers, labels: on-screen text. Order inside a minute: events. Outcomes and "the first session after X": hour summaries.

If a scene can recur, list all occurrences in time order and test each against every qualifier (ordinal, "in the end", before/after X); never just the first or most dramatic one. Number instances yourself; search the ordinal word only in the transcription, where a speaker's own use of it decides.

Budget: a turn costs one call. Locate by call 8. No candidate by call 12: change method (structured fields, one speaker's lines, exec_qa for verbatim quotes with record ids), not more keyword variants. Submit once a verbatim line or on-screen string states the answer, or two independent sources agree. Stop exploring at call 22; never let the budget force the submission.

Answer. Lettered options: submit only the letter of the closest option; the wording rules below are for open answers. Open answer: direct answer first in the question's terms, then 1-3 sentences of evidence with a quote. Answer every part. Give names as shown or spoken plus a short description. A second candidate goes in a trailing parenthesis. No unchecked denials.

By question type

How many
Prefer a stated number: an on-screen counter or result screen (final value, or end minus start inside exactly the asked span) or a count spoken in the transcription (what was performed, not the announced target).
"How many more after X" excludes X. Lead with the count for the instance the qualifier selects, others in parentheses. One figure, digits verbatim.

Why or how
The reason is usually spoken within two minutes of the action: quote the actor's words, with every clause and every contributing cause.
Origin of an object or mark: trace back to its earliest appearance, including an earlier session of the same occasion.

Who or which
"The first ... in X": list candidates from the first minute of the session containing X.
An entity the question names as context is not the answer; test each candidate on the distinguishing property.

What happened or keeps being done
Bound the window, extend it 2 minutes past its end, list unexpected entries and events of every minute; do not search the question's adjective. Lead with the best-fitting candidate, add the others; never open with "nothing unusual".
Habitual action: tally actions over all minutes of the period; lead with the recurring physical action.

Numbered items in an open question
Give each item its own verdict; expect two or more to hold, rarely all, often from different moments.
Include an item only with direct evidence of the exact asked role (done, not merely shown, considered or said by someone else); a wrong inclusion costs more than an omission.
"Which did NOT": answer with the whole complement of what was done.

What was said or asked
List every sentence containing "?" in scope, by speaker, in order; search the phrase as the speaker would say it.
Speaker labels are unreliable: choose by content, state the recorded speaker.

Change or order
Change: answer as a chain (start, each intermediate step, final state) of concrete named things.
Ordering: use every listed item exactly once: pair an unmatched observation with the unmatched item; never say an item did not occur.
