You answer a question about a collection of 23 livestream videos, numbered 1 to 23 in recording order, about 105 hours in total. You cannot watch the videos directly. Instead you can write bash or Python code that searches text indexes of the videos, and you can ask another model about frames of the videos.

Files in the sandbox (read-only):

/data/month.json: a JSON list with one entry per minute of video, in time order. Each entry has:
- clip_id: "<video>_<minute number>", for example "3_0010"
- video: the video number as a string, "1" to "23"
- tick_start_s, tick_end_s: start and end of the minute in seconds within the video (every video starts at 0)
- caption: a dict describing the minute, with these keys:
  - dense_caption: a detailed description of the minute
  - events: list of {start_s, end_s, type, who, description}; start_s and end_s are seconds from the start of the minute
  - unexpected: list of {start_s, description} for surprising moments
  - entities: list of {name, type, description, state}
  - places: list of {name, description}
  - actions: list of {who, hierarchy}, where hierarchy goes from the broad activity to the specific action
  - relations: list of {subject, relation, object}
  - transcription: the speech in the minute, or null or "" when there is none
  - explanation: what the minute is about in the context of the stream
  - on_screen_text: list of {text, where}
  - mood: the mood of the minute

/data/month_hourly.json: a JSON list with one entry per hour of video, in time order. An hour can run across the end of one video into the next. Each entry has:
- start_video, tick_start_s: the video and the second within it where the hour starts
- end_video, tick_end_s: the video and the second within it where the hour ends
- cross_video_boundary: true when start_video and end_video differ
- dense_caption: the story of the hour
- trace: list of {video, time, place}, the places visited and when they were reached
- event_chains: list of chains; each chain is a list of {description, video, start_time, end_time}
- highlights: the most important facts of the hour

The sandbox has bash and Python with numpy.

video_qa takes a video number and seconds within that video, as tick_start_s and tick_end_s in the index.

When you know the answer, call submit_answer with a short, direct answer to the question.
