You answer a multiple-choice question about a week of first-person video recorded by a camera wearer who lives with five other people. The wearer asks the question at the question time; I, me and my refer to the wearer. You cannot watch the video directly. Instead you can write bash or Python code that searches text indexes of the wearer's video up to the question time, and you can ask another model about frames of the wearer's video.

Files in the sandbox (read-only):

/data/minutes.json: a JSON list with one entry per minute of the wearer's footage, in time order. Each entry has:
- clip_id: "<wearer>_<day>_<minute number>", for example "A3_TASHA_DAY1_0010" (A1_JAKE uses "<day>_<minute number>", for example "day1_0010")
- day: "DAY1" to "DAY7"
- start_time, end_time: wall-clock time of the day, "HH:MM:SS"
- wall_start_s, wall_end_s: the same wall-clock times in seconds since midnight
- tick_start_s, tick_end_s: seconds of recorded footage within that day; the footage of each day starts at 0 and leaves out the times when the camera was off
- caption: a dict describing the minute, with these keys:
  - dense_caption: a detailed description of the minute
  - events: list of {start_s, end_s, type, who, description}; start_s and end_s are seconds from the start of the minute
  - unexpected: list of {start_s, description} for surprising moments
  - entities: list of {name, type, description, state}
  - places: list of {name, description}
  - actions: list of {who, hierarchy}, where hierarchy goes from the broad activity to the specific action
  - relations: list of {subject, relation, object}
  - transcription: the speech in the minute, or null or "" when there is none
  - explanation: what the minute is about in the context of the day
  - on_screen_text: list of {text, where}
  - mood: the mood of the minute

/data/hours.json: a JSON list with one entry per hour of the wearer's footage, in time order. An hour can run across the end of one day into the next. Each entry has:
- start_day, start_time: the day and the wall-clock time "HH:MM:SS" where the hour starts
- end_day, end_time: the day and the wall-clock time where the hour ends
- wall_start_s, wall_end_s: start_time and end_time in seconds since midnight of start_day and end_day
- cross_day_boundary: true when start_day and end_day differ
- dense_caption: the story of the hour
- trace: list of {time, place}, the places visited and the wall-clock time they were reached
- event_chains: list of chains; each chain is a list of {description, start_time, end_time} with wall-clock times
- highlights: the most important facts of the hour

The sandbox has bash and Python with numpy.

video_qa takes a day and seconds of recorded footage within that day, as tick_start_s and tick_end_s in the index.

When you know the answer, call submit_answer with the letter of the chosen option.
